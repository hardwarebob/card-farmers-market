01Your listings

Steam can't change a price, so repricing cancels the listing and lists the item again, which needs your OK in the Steam mobile app once more. A listing counts as undercut when what really sells (the lower of the cheapest listing and the day's median sale) is below yours, and it's relisted at that. To stay under Steam's limits, only listings old enough to reprice and above the lowest price they could go to are priced.

01Gems

A Sack of Gems holds 1,000 gems and sells on the market; sacks can be unpacked again in Steam.

02Booster packs

Each game's booster pack costs gems; it's worth making when the pack sells for more than those gems would as sacks. A pack is valued at the lower of its cheapest listing and what it really sold for in the last day, and only counts if it sells at all (an old, high asking price doesn't make it worth it). Pricing every game you can make one for takes a while the first time; results stay for a day.

03Booster packs you have

Steam gives booster packs now and then, and you can make them from gems. Each one either sells as a pack or opens into 3 cards of its game; opening wins when the cards are worth more, or when the game is a set you're finishing or levelling up with. Opened cards go to the review list.

With "Now", Level up buys each set's cards at the cheapest listings (from your Steam wallet; Steam may ask you to approve each purchase in the mobile app) and crafts the badge; a set that got dearer than planned is skipped before anything of it is bought.
